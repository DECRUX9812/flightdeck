# Three storylines

## A — "The tab you never open"  ← recommended
**Device:** life before and after. **Form:** one continuous take that pushes into and out of the console.
The viewer starts inside a live console UI, the camera pulls back to show it sitting alone on a dark
ground — nothing else on screen, no dashboards, no tabs — then dives back in and the console becomes
the whole film. It feels like finding a control room behind a door you walk past every day.

**First two seconds:** full-bleed console, numbers already ticking; at 0.4 s the camera begins to pull
back and the window resolves out of the dark.

**Beats:** live UI → pull back, "the console is already open" → "your dashboards aren't" → dive into
the wordmark → fleet → incidents → ⌘K → pull back to the whole window → claim → mark.

**Look:** product ground (#0a0a0a) with the window's own chrome; JetBrains Mono throughout; the only
colour outside the product is its accent #0070f3 on the command and the mark's own gradient.

**Sound:** minimal 140 BPM pulse; a low impact when the mark lands; key ticks only where keys are typed.

## B — "%c"
**Device:** type carries the argument. **Form:** kinetic type / cut on the beat.
A `console.log("%c…")` call streams its styled spans until the spans *are* the dashboard; every beat
adds a layer of the UI as type, then the type contracts into a single console window.

**First two seconds:** a raw format string at 200 px, `%c` marks flying past, one span turning blue.

**Beats:** raw log → spans assemble → the frame becomes a dashboard → the log collapses to a prompt.

**Look:** pure black, one monospace face, two inks (white and #0070f3), no photography.
**Sound:** machine rhythm, key ticks as percussion, no melody.

## C — "3:14 a.m."
**Device:** a moment in someone's day. **Form:** cut on the beat, one location.
A pager buzzes; a laptop opens to a page that is down; instead of six dashboards the engineer hits one
key and the console is already there. Cold blue light, one person's hands, the console reflected.

**First two seconds:** a phone lighting up on a dark desk, timestamp `03:14`.

**Beats:** page → tabs fail → console → one command → incident closed → back to bed.
**Look:** live-action desk, practical light, the console as the only screen that works.
**Sound:** room tone, a single sub hit on the page, silence under the fix.

> C needs footage we do not have (a person, a desk). B is the most striking but is type-only, which
> sells motion rather than the product. A shows the real thing performing — that is what gets shared.
